We help agencies deliver defined software work for their clients

You already own the client relationship. We build a clearly scoped piece of the project, or add agreed hours when you need more capacity, and hand the work back to you with its code and documentation.

What we do

We take on bounded software work that an agency, software firm or automation consultancy has already agreed with its own client. Typical examples:

Workflow automation. Replacing a repeated manual process, such as moving data between systems, generating documents or routing requests, with something that runs reliably on its own.

API integrations. Connecting systems that need to share data, including the error handling and logging that keep the connection working after launch.

Internal tools. Admin panels, review queues and small applications that a client’s staff use day to day.

Dashboards and reporting. Turning data a client already has into views people can act on.

Bounded web-app features. A defined feature or module inside an existing web application, built to that project’s conventions.

These are useful starting points, not the limit of what we build. If the work is well defined, send it over, and we’ll tell you whether it’s a good fit for us.

Two ways to work together

A fixed-scope task. One defined deliverable with written acceptance criteria. It’s finished when it meets them.

Agreed capacity. A set number of hours, agreed in advance, for ongoing work on your project and directed by your team.

Scope, hours and pricing for either are agreed in writing for each engagement.

How an engagement could work

Further work follows only if the pilot shows it’s a good fit for both of us.

  1. 01

    You send a brief

    We read it, ask what we need to, and tell you whether it’s a good technical fit.

  2. 02

    We agree scope and acceptance criteria

    In writing: what will be built, what “done” means, what’s out of scope, and who on your side signs off.

  3. 03

    We start with a small paid pilot

    A first task, scoped and priced on its own, so both sides can see how working together goes before committing to more.

  4. 04

    You review it in a test or staging environment

    We agree that environment with you before anything reaches production.

  5. 05

    We hand over code and documentation

    Into the repository agreed at the start, with the notes your team needs to run and change it.

Your client, your relationship

The client relationship stays yours. Before work starts, we agree with you:

Who talks to the client. Whether we work only through you, join calls at your direction, or speak with the client directly.

How the work is presented. We follow what you’ve agreed with your client. If that agreement requires subcontractors to be disclosed or approved, that requirement comes first.

The terms, in writing. Confidentiality, intellectual property, repository ownership, access to systems and data, handover, and how fixes after delivery are handled are all agreed in writing before work starts.

Where the work runs. Where code runs and what data we can see are part of the scope, agreed with you and, where needed, with your client.

Custom work, not resold products. We build to your specification. We don’t rebrand someone else’s product and sell it as our own work, and that holds when we work for an agency too.
